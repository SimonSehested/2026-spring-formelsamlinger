\section{Nyquist plot and stability}

\subsection{Nyquist curve}

\subsubsection{Nyquist plot definition}
\[
\Gamma=\{L(j\omega):\omega\in(-\infty,\infty)\}
\]
The Nyquist plot maps the open-loop frequency response into the complex plane.

\subsubsection{Critical point}
\[
-1+j0
\]
Closed-loop stability is assessed by the relation between the Nyquist curve and the critical point.

\subsection{Nyquist stability criterion}

\subsubsection{Encirclement equation}
\[
Z=P+N
\]
Here \(P\) is the number of open-loop right-half-plane poles, \(N\) is the net clockwise encirclements of \(-1\), and \(Z\) is the number of closed-loop right-half-plane poles. % TODO: verify sign convention from source

\subsubsection{Closed-loop stability condition}
\[
Z=0
\]
The closed loop is stable when the Nyquist encirclements compensate the open-loop unstable poles so that no closed-loop right-half-plane poles remain.

\subsubsection{Stable open-loop special case}
\[
P=0,\qquad N=0
\]
If the open-loop system is stable, the Nyquist curve must not encircle the point \(-1+j0\).

\subsection{Margins from Nyquist}

\subsubsection{Phase margin from unit circle intersection}
\[
|L(j\omega_c)|=1,\qquad
\gamma_M=180^\circ+\arg L(j\omega_c)
\]
On a Nyquist plot, \(\omega_c\) is where the curve intersects the unit circle.

\subsubsection{Gain margin from negative real-axis intersection}
\[
L(j\omega_\pi)=-a,\qquad A_M=\frac{1}{a}
\]
The gain margin is read where the Nyquist curve crosses the negative real axis.

\subsubsection{P-controller scaling in Nyquist}
\[
L_K(j\omega)=K_P L(j\omega)
\]
Changing \(K_P\) radially scales the Nyquist curve. The critical gain occurs when the scaled curve passes through \(-1+j0\).
