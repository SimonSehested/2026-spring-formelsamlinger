\section{Atoms, Stoichiometry, and Nomenclature}

\subsection{Atoms and Isotopes}

\subsubsection{Atomic number, mass number, and isotope notation}
\[
{}^{A}_{Z}\ce{X}, \qquad A=Z+N
\]
\(Z\) is the number of protons, \(N\) is the number of neutrons, and \(A\) is the mass number. Isotopes have the same \(Z\) and different \(N\).

\subsubsection{Subatomic particles}
\[
\begin{array}{c c c}
\text{particle} & \text{relative charge} & \text{approximate mass}\\
\hline
\text{electron} & -1 & 9.1\times 10^{-28}\,\mathrm{g}\\
\text{proton} & +1 & 1.7\times 10^{-24}\,\mathrm{g}\\
\text{neutron} & 0 & 1.7\times 10^{-24}\,\mathrm{g}
\end{array}
\]
Atomic charge is determined by proton and electron count. Atomic mass is dominated by protons and neutrons.

\subsection{Amounts of Substance}

\subsubsection{Avogadro constant and mole}
\[
N=nN_{\mathrm A}, \qquad N_{\mathrm A}=6.022\times 10^{23}\,\mathrm{mol^{-1}}
\]
The mole converts between a count of particles \(N\) and an amount of substance \(n\).

\subsubsection{Molar mass relation}
\[
n=\frac{m}{M}, \qquad m=nM
\]
\(m\) is mass, \(M\) is molar mass, and \(n\) is amount of substance. This relation is central for converting grams to reaction amounts.

\subsubsection{Mass percent}
\[
w_i=\frac{m_i}{m_{\mathrm{total}}}\times 100\%
\]
Mass percent gives the mass fraction of a component in a compound or mixture.

\subsubsection{Empirical and molecular formula}
\[
\text{empirical formula ratio}=\frac{n_i}{\min(n_i)}, \qquad
\text{molecular formula}=k(\text{empirical formula})
\]
The empirical formula gives the smallest integer atom ratio. The molecular formula also requires the molar mass.

\subsection{Stoichiometry}

\subsubsection{Balanced reaction coefficients}
\[
\nu_A\ce{A}+\nu_B\ce{B}\rightarrow \nu_C\ce{C}+\nu_D\ce{D}
\]
Balanced coefficients conserve atoms and charge. Mole ratios in calculations are the ratios of the stoichiometric coefficients.

\subsubsection{Limiting reactant}
\[
\frac{n_i}{\nu_i}
\]
The smallest value of \(n_i/\nu_i\) identifies the limiting reactant. Product amounts are obtained by multiplying this reaction extent by product coefficients.

\subsubsection{Theoretical yield}
\[
n_{\mathrm{product}}=\frac{\nu_{\mathrm{product}}}{\nu_{\mathrm{limiting}}}n_{\mathrm{limiting}}, \qquad
m_{\mathrm{product}}=n_{\mathrm{product}}M_{\mathrm{product}}
\]
The theoretical yield is the maximum product amount allowed by the balanced equation.

\subsection{Chemical Formulae and Names}

\subsubsection{Ionic compound charge neutrality}
\[
\sum_i q_i n_i=0
\]
The formula of an ionic compound must have total charge zero. Parentheses are used when a polyatomic ion appears more than once, for example \(\ce{Ca(HSO4)2}\).

\[
\begin{array}{c c@{\qquad}c c}

\midrule
\ce{NH4+} & \text{ammonium} & \ce{NO3-} & \text{nitrate}\\
\ce{NO2-} & \text{nitrite} & \ce{SO4^2-} & \text{sulfate}\\
\ce{HSO4-} & \text{hydrogen sulfate} & \ce{SO3^2-} & \text{sulfite}\\
\ce{CO3^2-} & \text{carbonate} & \ce{PO4^3-} & \text{phosphate}\\

\bottomrule
\end{array}
\]
These ions occur repeatedly in nomenclature, acid--base, redox, and solubility questions.

\subsubsection{Molecular compound Greek prefixes}
\[
\begin{array}{c c@{\quad}c c@{\quad}c c}
\toprule
1&\text{mono} & 2&\text{di} & 3&\text{tri}\\
4&\text{tetra} & 5&\text{penta} & 6&\text{hexa}\\

\end{array}
\]
For binary molecular compounds, the first element is named first and the second receives the ending ``-ide''.
