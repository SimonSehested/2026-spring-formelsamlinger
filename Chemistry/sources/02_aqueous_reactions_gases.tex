\section{Aqueous Reactions, Redox Balancing, and Gases}

\subsection{Ctrl+F keywords: molarity, dilution, net ionic, precipitation, redox, oxidation number}
Keywords continued: gas law, partial pressure, idealgas, gas stoichiometry, pressure tank.

\subsection{Solutions and aqueous reactions}

\subsubsection{Molarity, dilution, actual ion concentration}
\[
c=\frac{n}{V}, \qquad n=cV, \qquad c_1V_1=c_2V_2
\]
Use liters for \(V\). For strong electrolytes, multiply by the ion coefficient: \(\ce{CaCl2 -> Ca^2+ + 2Cl-}\) gives \([\ce{Cl-}]=2c\).

\subsubsection{Net ionic equation and spectator ions}
\[
\ce{Pb^2+(aq) + 2I-(aq) -> PbI2(s)}
\]
Remove ions that appear unchanged on both sides. Net ionic equations must conserve atoms and charge.

\subsubsection{Precipitation recognition}
\[
Q_{sp}>K_{sp}\Rightarrow\text{precipitate}, \qquad Q_{sp}<K_{sp}\Rightarrow\text{no precipitate yet}
\]
Exam wording often says ``mixing solutions'', ``sparingly soluble'', ``bundfald'', or gives \(K_{sp}\).

\subsection{Exam recipe: net ionic precipitation reaction, bundfald}
\textbf{Use when:} two aqueous ionic solutions are mixed and a solid product is possible.

\textbf{Steps:}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Dissociate strong aqueous electrolytes into ions.
    \item Pair cations and anions to identify possible insoluble products.
    \item Write the solid with phase \((s)\).
    \item Cancel spectator ions.
    \item Check atom and charge balance.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Common traps:} leaving nitrate or alkali metal spectator ions in the net equation; forgetting ion charges when writing formulas.

\subsection{Redox balancing}

\subsubsection{Oxidation and reduction half-reactions}
\[
\text{oxidation: electrons produced}, \qquad
\text{reduction: electrons consumed}
\]
Oxidation state increases during oxidation and decreases during reduction. Electron counts must cancel in the total reaction.

\subsubsection{Half-reaction balancing in acid or base}
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{balance atoms except H,O},\\
&\text{balance O with }\ce{H2O},\\
&\text{balance H with }\ce{H+},\\
&\text{balance charge with }\ce{e-},\\
&\text{for base: add }\ce{OH-}\text{ to neutralize }\ce{H+}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The final equation must have no free electrons and must conserve total charge.

\subsection{Exam recipe: balance redox equation in acidic or basic solution}
\textbf{Use when:} the problem asks to balance \(\ce{MnO4-}\), oxalate, iodide, sulfite, chromium, or other redox reactions.

\textbf{Steps:}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Split into oxidation and reduction half-reactions.
    \item Balance non-\(\ce{H}\), non-\(\ce{O}\) atoms.
    \item Balance \(\ce{O}\) with \(\ce{H2O}\) and \(\ce{H}\) with \(\ce{H+}\).
    \item Balance charge with electrons.
    \item Multiply half-reactions so electrons cancel and add.
    \item In basic solution, add \(\ce{OH-}\) to both sides for each \(\ce{H+}\), form \(\ce{H2O}\), then cancel water.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Fast checks:} same total charge and same atom count on both sides.

\subsection{Gases}

\subsubsection{Ideal gas law and combined gas law}
\[
pV=nRT, \qquad \frac{p_1V_1}{T_1}=\frac{p_2V_2}{T_2}\quad(n\ \text{constant})
\]
Temperatures must be in kelvin. Use a gas constant matching pressure units.

\subsubsection{Partial pressure and mole fraction}
\[
p_i=x_ip_{\mathrm{tot}}, \qquad x_i=\frac{n_i}{n_{\mathrm{tot}}}, \qquad p_{\mathrm{tot}}=\sum_i p_i
\]
Dalton's law applies to ideal gas mixtures. Mole fraction equals pressure fraction.

\subsubsection{Gas density and molar mass}
\[
\rho=\frac{m}{V}=\frac{pM}{RT}, \qquad M=\frac{\rho RT}{p}
\]
This is the fastest route when density, pressure, and temperature of a gas are given.

\subsection{Exam recipe: gas stoichiometry, gas volume, pressure tank}
\textbf{Use when:} gas volume, pressure, temperature, or a heated tank appears in a stoichiometry or state-change problem.

\textbf{Steps:}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Convert temperature to kelvin and volume to liters or cubic meters consistently.
    \item Use \(pV=nRT\) to find moles, or the combined gas law if \(n\) is fixed.
    \item For reactions, use balanced coefficients after converting gas data to moles.
    \item Convert final moles to requested mass, volume, pressure, or yield.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Common traps:} Celsius in gas laws; using total pressure where partial pressure is required; forgetting gas stoichiometric coefficients.
