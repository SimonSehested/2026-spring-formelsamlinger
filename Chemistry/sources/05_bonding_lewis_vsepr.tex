\section{Chemical Bonds, Lewis Structures, and VSEPR}

\subsection{Bond Types}

\subsubsection{Ionic bonding and Coulomb stabilization}
\[
E\propto \frac{q_1q_2}{r}
\]
Ionic solids are stabilized by electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions. Lattice enthalpy can make salt formation strongly favorable.

\subsubsection{Born-Haber cycle}
\[
\Delta H^\circ_{\mathrm f}=\sum \Delta H^\circ_{\mathrm{atomization/ionization/electron\ affinity}}+\Delta H^\circ_{\mathrm{lattice}}
\]
A Born-Haber cycle applies Hess's law to ionic solid formation. Large negative lattice enthalpy stabilizes the crystal.

\subsubsection{Covalent bond polarity}
\[
\Delta \chi=|\chi_A-\chi_B|
\]
Greater electronegativity difference gives a more polar bond. Molecular polarity also depends on geometry.

\subsubsection{Molecular orbital bond order}
\[
\text{bond order}=\frac{N_{\mathrm{bonding}}-N_{\mathrm{antibonding}}}{2}
\]
Bond order estimates bond strength and whether a simple diatomic molecule is stabilized.

\subsection{Lewis Structures}

\subsubsection{Valence electron count}
\[
N_{\mathrm{val}}=\sum_i N_{\mathrm{val},i}-q
\]
For an ion, subtract positive charge and add negative charge. The total electron count controls the Lewis structure.

\subsubsection{Formal charge}
\[
\mathrm{FC}=V-\left(N_{\mathrm{nonbonding}}+\frac{1}{2}N_{\mathrm{bonding}}\right)
\]
Formal charge compares assigned electrons to neutral valence electrons. Favored resonance forms usually minimize charge and place negative charge on electronegative atoms.

\subsubsection{Octet rule and expanded octet}
\[
\text{second-period atoms normally obey an octet}
\]
Atoms from period 3 and below may appear with expanded octets in course examples such as \(\ce{SF6}\). Electron-deficient molecules such as \(\ce{BH3}\) are exceptions.

\subsubsection{Resonance structures}
\[
\text{real structure}=\text{resonance hybrid of valid Lewis forms}
\]
Resonance forms differ only in electron placement, not atom positions. Equivalent resonance forms imply delocalized bonding and equalized bond lengths.

\subsection{VSEPR Geometry}

\subsubsection{Electron-density domains}
\[
\text{steric number}=\text{number of bonded atoms}+\text{number of lone pairs}
\]
VSEPR arranges electron domains to maximize separation around the central atom.

\subsubsection{Common VSEPR geometries}
\[
\begin{array}{c c c c}
\toprule
\text{steric number} & \text{lone pairs} & \text{molecular geometry} & \text{example}\\
\midrule
2 & 0 & \text{linear} & \ce{CO2}\\
3 & 0 & \text{trigonal planar} & \ce{BH3}\\
4 & 0 & \text{tetrahedral} & \ce{CH4}\\
4 & 1 & \text{trigonal pyramidal} & \ce{NH3}\\
4 & 2 & \text{bent} & \ce{H2O}\\
5 & 0 & \text{trigonal bipyramidal} & \ce{PCl5}\\
6 & 0 & \text{octahedral} & \ce{SF6}\\
6 & 1 & \text{square pyramidal} & \ce{IF5}\\
5 & 3 & \text{linear} & \ce{XeF2}\\
\bottomrule
\end{array}
\]
The exam wording often asks for planar, bent, pyramidal, tetrahedral, or octahedral geometry.

\subsubsection{Representative bond angles}
\[
\begin{array}{c c}
\toprule
\text{geometry} & \text{ideal angle}\\
\midrule
\text{linear} & 180^\circ\\
\text{trigonal planar} & 120^\circ\\
\text{tetrahedral} & 109.5^\circ\\
\text{trigonal pyramidal} & \approx 107^\circ\\
\text{bent water-like} & \approx 105^\circ\\
\text{octahedral} & 90^\circ\\
\bottomrule
\end{array}
\]
Lone pairs compress bonding angles relative to ideal electron-domain angles.

\subsubsection{Molecular polarity from bond dipoles}
\[
\vec{\mu}_{\mathrm{mol}}=\sum_i \vec{\mu}_i
\]
Polar bonds can cancel in symmetric molecules. Molecular polarity depends on both electronegativity and molecular shape.
