Continuous time signal:
Discrete time signals:
Analog signals are continuous both in amplitude and
time. Analog signals are also often called continuous-time
(CT) signals. Discrete-time (DT) signals are defined only
at certain (often constant) time intervals. The samples of
DT signals are assumed to have continuous amplitude
values. A digital signal is discrete both in time and in
amplitude, i.e., the amplitude of each sample is
represented by a finite number of bits.

 Power signal: A signal with finite power
 A periodic signal with finite energy in one period will have infinite energy in all periods
 Thus a periodic signal is never an energy signal.
 Signals with infinite power is neither an energy nor a power signal

functions.

Causal Systems
A system is causal if its output depends only on present and past input.
A noncausal system can have output that depend on future input. It must predict future values of the
input signal. A noncausal system can have an output before the input is applied, i.e., without ”cause”.
Physical systems are always causal.
Noncausal systems are only of theoretical interest.
